# DEVLOG: options pricing, volatility surfaces and hedging on one day of Deribit

### What I wanted to build

I wanted the whole path from raw option quotes to a hedged book, on real data, with every step measured: price options, back out implied vols fast and exactly, clean a crypto option chain properly, fit a volatility surface and check it for arbitrage, check my numbers against the exchange's own, and then ask a practical trading question. If I sell short-dated options and delta hedge them with the future, how much does the hedged P&L still wobble, and does it matter whether I hedge every minute or every half hour, or which delta I use?

The data is one day of Deribit BTC and ETH options, 2026-09-01, from the tardis.dev free first-of-month sample of the `options_chain` dataset. That day has 12 BTC and 12 ETH expiries, from same day (08:00 UTC) out to June 2027.

### Theory

Pricing. Deribit options are European on the expiry's forward F, so I use Black-76: `C = D (F N(d1) - K N(d2))`, `d1 = (ln(F/K) + v^2/2) / v`, `v = sigma sqrt(T)`. Deribit quotes premiums in coin; the USD value is the coin premium times the forward. The Greeks I need are with respect to F and sigma: delta `N(d1)`, gamma `phi(d1) / (F v)`, vega `F phi(d1) sqrt(T)`, theta `-F phi(d1) sigma / (2 sqrt(T))` with a zero rate, vanna `-phi(d1) d2 / sigma`, volga `vega d1 d2 / sigma`.

Implied vol. Price is monotone in v between the no-arbitrage bounds `intrinsic <= C <= F` (and `<= K` for puts), so a unique vol exists inside the bounds and none outside. Two ideas make a robust solver: always invert the out-of-the-money price (put-call parity `C - P = D (F - K)` converts an ITM price), and do Newton on `log(price)` rather than price, which is nearly linear in v for far OTM options where the price is tiny.

Forward. With coin premiums and a zero rate, parity reads `C_coin - P_coin = 1 - K / F`. Regressing `C - P` on K across strikes gives intercept `a` near 1 and slope `b = -1/F`, so `F = -a / b`. The intercept is a free sanity check.

Surface. Raw SVI models total variance `w(k) = sigma^2 T` in log moneyness `k = ln(K/F)`: `w = a + b (rho (k - m) + sqrt((k - m)^2 + s^2))`. A slice is free of butterfly arbitrage when the Gatheral-Jacquier function `g(k) = (1 - k w'/(2w))^2 - (w'^2/4)(1/w + 1/4) + w''/2` is non-negative everywhere (it is proportional to the risk neutral density). The surface is free of calendar arbitrage when `w(k, T)` never decreases in T at fixed k. SSVI writes the whole surface as `w = theta/2 (1 + rho phi k + sqrt((phi k + rho)^2 + 1 - rho^2))` with `phi(theta) = eta / (theta^gamma (1 + theta)^(1 - gamma))`, and it is free of static arbitrage under simple parameter conditions, at the price of only three shape parameters for all expiries.

Hedging. Over a small step a delta hedged short option earns `-(theta dt + 1/2 gamma dF^2 + vega dsigma) + (h - delta) dF` plus higher order terms. With `theta = -1/2 gamma F^2 sigma^2`, the first two terms are `1/2 gamma F^2 (sigma_implied^2 - sigma_realized^2) dt`: selling vol makes money when realized comes in below implied. Discrete hedging adds noise from the stale hedge `(h - delta) dF`. Which delta to use depends on how the smile moves with F: sticky strike keeps `sigma(K)` fixed (delta is Black-76 delta at the surface vol), sticky moneyness keeps `sigma(k)` fixed, adding `vega * dsigma/dF = -vega sigma'(k) / F`.

### Architecture

```
tardis CSV.gz -> curl | gunzip -> minute_snap (C++) -> 1 row / symbol / minute (147 MB)
   -> chain.py (load, snapshot, clean, parity forward, IVs) -> surface.py + svi.py (fits, checks)
   -> hedging.py (minute panels, SVI path, hedge ratios, simulate, attribute, bootstrap)
black76.py and iv.py (numba) sit under all of it
```

The one design decision that shaped everything else: the raw day is about 100 GB of CSV (236M rows), so I never store it. A small C++ program sits in the download pipe, keeps BTC and ETH rows, and emits per symbol only the last update before each minute boundary. That turns the day into 2.3M rows that polars loads in under 2 seconds, while keeping enough resolution to hedge every minute. Everything downstream is either a polars frame (for snapshot style work across strikes and expiries) or a dense numpy panel of minutes by strikes (for the hedging simulation). DESIGN.md has the full data flow and invariants.

### Implementation

Black-76 and IV are numba kernels with `prange` over options. The CDF uses `erfc` rather than `0.5 (1 + erf)` so the lower tail keeps relative precision. The IV kernel normalizes to `F = 1`, checks bounds and returns a status code plus NaN when the price is outside them, converts to the OTM price, takes the Corrado-Miller closed form as the first guess (or the inflection point `sqrt(2 |ln K/F|)` when its discriminant is negative), then runs Newton on `log(price)` in total vol while keeping a bracket; any Newton step leaving the bracket becomes a bisection. Anything still unconverged after 40 steps is re-solved with scipy's Brent on the original price.

Chain cleaning (`chain.py`) takes an as-of snapshot (last row per symbol at or before t, dropping quotes older than 10 minutes), drops one-sided, zero-bid, crossed and locked quotes, computes mids and exact T from the 08:00 UTC expiry timestamp (the loader asserts every expiry is at 08:00), fits the parity forward per expiry with a weighted least squares on strikes within 25% of the money plus one robust pass that drops pairs more than 4 MAD out, and attaches bid, mid and ask IVs with their statuses.

SVI (`svi.py`) fits total variance with `scipy.optimize.least_squares` (bounded trust region) from 6 starts built from the smile's minimum and wing slopes. I reparameterize `a` as `amin - b s sqrt(1 - rho^2)` so that "minimum variance is non-negative" is a plain bound `amin >= 0`, bound `|rho| < 1`, `b >= 0`, `s > 0`, and add Roger Lee's wing bound `b (1 + |rho|) <= 2` as a hinge penalty. Weights are vega over bid-ask spread, capped at 10x the slice median. The butterfly check evaluates g on 2001 points spanning the quoted k range plus half its width on each side; the calendar check compares adjacent expiries on the overlap of their quoted ranges. SSVI is fit to all of a coin's expiries at once with `theta` as cumulative sums of exponentials (so it is increasing), `rho = tanh(x)`, `gamma` squashed into (0, 1/2), `eta` squashed below `2 / (1 + |rho|)`.

Hedging (`hedging.py`) builds a minute panel for one expiry with an as-of join, fits SVI every minute (warm started from the previous minute), and computes three hedge ratios: Black-76 delta at the option's own mid IV, at the SVI vol (sticky strike), and the SVI delta plus the skew term (sticky moneyness). `simulate` shorts one option at mid, holds the hedge fixed between rebalances, and splits each minute's P&L into theta, gamma, vega, hedge error and residual using Greeks at own IV at the start of the minute. `bootstrap_std` resamples options, or whole windows when positions share a window, 2000 times.

Four experiment scripts produce everything in results/, and `scripts/run_all.sh` runs the tests plus all four in about 2 minutes with thread counts capped at 2.

### Problems

- The download was the first wall. Each tardis day is 8 to 12 GB gzipped and about 100 GB of text, too much to store and too much for a line-by-line awk or Python filter to keep up with, so I wrote `minute_snap` in C++ and put it inside the curl pipe. The 2026-09-01 day took about 20 minutes end to end. A second day (2026-08-01) was stopped before it finished when the shared machine had to shed load, so v0 is one day.
- Numba warned on every call that it was reading a read-only broadcast view. `np.broadcast_arrays` returns read-only views and even asking for `flags.writeable` triggers the deprecation. I replaced the broadcasting helper with one that returns arrays already at the target shape untouched and materializes only the ones that actually need expanding.
- There is a 167 minute hole in the sample, 18:52 to 21:39 UTC, with no rows at all. My 10 minute staleness filter correctly produced empty snapshots at 20:00 and 21:00, which first showed up as two hours that added zero slices. The experiments now skip empty snapshots and record them (`empty_hours` in `results/02_surface_summary.json`), and the rolling hedging windows stop before the gap.
- The 08:00 UTC snapshot is the daily settlement and the book is gutted: 943 one-sided quotes versus 104 at 09:00, and only 747 clean quotes kept versus about 1690 in a normal hour (`results/02_drop_counts.csv`). That hour produced both the worst SVI fits of the day (two ETH long-dated slices with 20.8 and 38.1 vol point RMSE) and a bug: the ETH December parity regression was accepted from only 3 pairs with an intercept of 0.73, when the coin convention says it must be near 1. I now accept a parity forward only with at least 5 pairs and `|a - 1| <= 0.01`, otherwise fall back to Deribit's underlying, with a test for it. After the fix, 95% of expiry snapshots use the parity forward and the largest gap to Deribit's underlying fell from 29.4 bp to 9.7 bp.
- In the hedging experiment the first attribution was nonsense for in-the-money options: vega and residual terms of plus and minus 200 bp that cancelled each other. An ITM option's mid carries the spread of a large premium, so its implied vol swings wildly minute to minute, and `vega * dsigma` with huge jumps is not a Taylor term any more. I give each ITM option the IV of its OTM twin at the same strike (parity says they are equal), and fall back to the surface when the twin has no quote.
- The settlement minute also broke one hedging window. At exactly 08:00 an ETH put's half spread went from 0.37 USD to 57.85 USD for one minute, and an ITM BTC call's mid sat about 320 USD above its parity value. Neither is crossed, so cleaning kept them, and selling at those mids booked fake profits of up to 230 bp. I added a spike filter (drop a quote whose half spread is more than 10x that option's median over the panel) and restricted the hedging book to OTM options: an ITM option is its OTM twin plus a forward, the forward is hedged away, so ITM positions only add noisier quotes. That removed the outliers.
- In the last minutes before expiry, gamma and theta blow up and a one-minute Taylor expansion stops meaning anything. I report the final 30 minutes (which is also Deribit's settlement averaging window) as a single `tail` term instead of forcing it into theta and gamma.
- One of my own tests was wrong. I asserted that with negative rho the sticky moneyness delta is always above the sticky strike delta. It is only above where the smile slopes down; SVI's right wing still slopes up with `rho = -0.7`. The test now checks both sides.
- A number that looked like a bug but was not: the benchmark summary printed a round-trip error of exactly 0. My print rounded to 4 decimals; the JSON has 1.1e-15.

### Experiments

1. IV and pricer benchmark (`experiments/01_iv_benchmark.py`, output `results/01_iv_benchmark.json`). 2M random options, log moneyness in [-1, 1], T from one hour to three years, vol 2% to 300%, calls and puts. Price, compute Greeks, invert, reprice, on 1 thread and on 2 threads, best of 5 timed runs.
2. Surface fits (`experiments/02_surface_fits.py`, outputs `results/02_svi_slices.csv`, `02_forwards.csv`, `02_drop_counts.csv`, `02_surface_summary.json`, plots `02_smiles_btc.png`, `02_smiles_eth.png`, `02_svi_vs_ssvi_rmse.png`). Hourly snapshots 00:00 to 23:00 UTC, both coins, SVI on every expiry with at least 5 clean OTM quotes, both arbitrage checks, and SSVI per coin per hour.
3. Own IV against Deribit mark IV (`experiments/03_mark_iv_compare.py`, outputs `results/03_mark_iv_buckets.csv`, `03_mark_iv_tenor.csv`, `03_mark_iv_outliers.csv`, `03_mark_iv_summary.json`, `03_mark_iv_diff.png`). Every clean quote with a valid mid IV in the hourly snapshots, 32,624 quotes. The difference `iv_mid - mark_iv` is split exactly into a price part (my mid vs Deribit's mark price, both on my forward), a forward part (my parity forward vs Deribit's underlying, both on the mark price) and a residual (the IV of Deribit's mark price on Deribit's underlying minus Deribit's mark IV). Buckets are standardized moneyness `z = k / (sigma sqrt T)` and time to expiry.
4. Hedging (`experiments/04_hedging.py`, outputs `results/04_hedge_pnl.csv`, `04_hedge_summary.csv`, `04_hedge_context.json`, `04_hedge_std.png`, `04_attribution.png`). Experiment A: at 00:05 UTC sell every OTM same day option (expiring 08:00) with a clean quote and `|delta|` in [0.05, 0.95], 7 BTC and 6 ETH, hold to settlement against the mean underlying over 07:30 to 07:59. Experiment B: on the next day's expiry, sell every OTM option with `|delta|` in [0.10, 0.90] at the top of each hour from 08:00 to 14:00, hold 4 hours and close at mid, 101 positions over 14 coin windows. Each position is hedged every 1, 5 and 30 minutes with each of the three deltas, plus an unhedged baseline. P&L is in basis points of the entry forward. Bootstrap: 2000 resamples of options in A and of whole (coin, window) clusters in B.

### Results

IV solver. 2.07M implied vols per second on one thread and 4.07M on two, including Brent fallbacks; pricing runs at 21M options/s and full Greeks at 19M/s on one thread. The maximum round-trip price error is 1.1e-15 of F (p99 2.8e-16), against a 1e-10 target, and where vega exceeds 1e-3 the vol itself comes back within 5.6e-11. Newton takes 6.2 steps on average, 12 at p99. 493 of 2M options (0.025%) went to Brent and none failed; 2.0% returned `AT_INTRINSIC` because their time value is below double precision relative to intrinsic. All from `results/01_iv_benchmark.json`. The Greeks match central finite differences to 1e-6 relative over 300 hypothesis cases (`tests/test_black76.py`).

Cleaning and forwards. Over 22 non-empty hourly snapshots, 34,783 quotes survived and 3,391 were one-sided or empty; tardis encodes a missing bid as empty, so the zero-bid and crossed counters stayed at 0. The accepted parity forwards sit a median 1.66 bp from Deribit's underlying (max 9.7 bp) with median intercept 0.9998 (`results/02_surface_summary.json`).

Surfaces. Across 501 SVI slices the median fit RMSE is 0.29 vol points against a median half spread of 0.68 vol points; 95.2% of slices fit inside their own half spread, and on average 98.6% of a slice's fitted vols land between the bid and ask IV. Error shrinks with maturity, from 0.54 vp under one day to 0.13 vp beyond 90 days (`results/02_svi_vs_ssvi_rmse.png`). 98.2% of slices pass the butterfly check, 98.0% the calendar check, 96.4% both. The 18 failures explain themselves when I look at them (`results/02_svi_slices.csv`): every butterfly violation has its minimum g outside the quoted strike range, in the extrapolated left wing, so it is an extrapolation artifact rather than a mispriced quote. Four of the five calendar violations are between the Friday 4 Sep and Saturday 5 Sep BTC expiries, where one extra calendar day is mostly weekend and adds almost no variance, so noise makes the two slices cross by about 7e-5 in total variance. The fifth is the gutted 08:00 ETH long end. SSVI is arbitrage free on every slice by construction, and it pays for it: median RMSE 1.89 vp, 6.6 times worse than per-expiry SVI, and worse than the half spread in every maturity bucket except under one day. One set of three shape parameters cannot hold both the steep short-dated BTC wings and the flatter long end, as the smile plots show.

Mark IV. My mid IVs agree with Deribit's mark IV to a median of 0.010 vp over all 32,624 quotes and 0.012 vp over the 16,390 OTM quotes, with a median absolute deviation of 0.091 vp on OTM quotes, and 99.1% of OTM differences are inside the quote's own half spread (`results/03_mark_iv_summary.json`). By moneyness the agreement is tightest near the money (ATM bucket median -0.005 vp, MAD 0.073 vp) and loosens in the wings (z below -2: median 0.14 vp, MAD 1.0 vp; z above 2: 0.38 vp and 1.4 vp), and by maturity it goes from MAD 1.35 vp under one day to 0.09 vp beyond 90 days (`results/03_mark_iv_buckets.csv`, `03_mark_iv_tenor.csv`). The difference correlates 0.80 with the price part, so most disagreement is simply my mid versus Deribit's mark price. The large outliers (`results/03_mark_iv_outliers.csv`) are almost all deep in-the-money same day options an hour or two before expiry, with under 0.001 coin of time value: tiny vega means any price difference becomes hundreds of vol points, and for these the residual is also large, meaning Deribit's own mark price and mark IV are not consistent with each other under Black-76 at its published underlying. Two outliers are longer dated (BTC 25SEP26 84000 put and ETH 4SEP26 2400 call at 08:00) and sit entirely in the price part, which is the settlement minute's wide quotes again.

Hedging. Delta hedging does most of the work, and the choice of delta barely matters on this day (`results/04_hedge_summary.csv`, `results/04_hedge_std.png`).

Hedging cuts the dispersion of P&L by a factor of 4 in both experiments. Rebalancing less often raises it steadily, from 2.5 to 4.0 bp in A and 3.9 to 4.8 bp in B with the own IV delta, and the attribution shows why: the hedge error term grows from 0 to 2.6 bp (A) and 0 to 4.0 bp (B) std while the other terms stay put (`results/04_attribution.png`). Between hedge ratios, own IV and sticky strike are nearly identical in B because the SVI fit sits within a fraction of a vol point of each quote; in A sticky strike is the worst at every interval and sticky moneyness sits in between, but all of these confidence intervals overlap. With 13 positions on one price path in A and 14 windows in B, this day cannot separate the three deltas.

The attribution also reads as a small volatility risk premium story. In A both coins realized less than implied (BTC 29.4% realized vs 33.8% ATM IV at entry, ETH 32.3% vs 40.3%, `results/04_hedge_context.json`), and the hedged short book earned a mean 4.1 bp with 1 minute own IV hedging: theta paid 12.2 bp, gamma cost 9.7 bp. In B the afternoon moved more than the market priced (window averages BTC 43.6% realized vs 33.6% implied, ETH 47.6% vs 41.6%), gamma cost 8.1 bp against 5.2 bp of theta, and the 1 minute hedged mean was -0.7 bp. Vega is the largest single source of dispersion in B at 4.6 bp std, because those positions are closed at mid four hours later and IV moves in between; in A the options run to settlement so vega mostly washes out (1.9 bp).

### What I would change

- Run the hedging study on many days. One day gives one price path per coin, so the intervals above are about which options and windows I happened to pick, not about hedging in general. The pipeline already takes the day as an argument; the constraint was download time on a shared machine.
- Build the surface from the settlement-aware data. The 08:00 minute should be flagged as a regime, not cleaned like any other minute, and the snapshot and window times should avoid it by design.
- Constrain the SVI fit to be butterfly free on the quoted range (penalize negative g on the grid) and use a bounded extrapolation outside it, rather than only flagging violations that all turned out to be in the extrapolated wing.
- Price and hedge in coin, the way Deribit margins it, and add half spread costs for the option and the future. Once costs are in, the rebalancing frequency result will stop being monotone, which is the interesting version of the question.
- Replace the SSVI comparison with an eSSVI or per-slice SSVI with a shared rho, which keeps the arbitrage guarantees but gives short expiries their own skew.
